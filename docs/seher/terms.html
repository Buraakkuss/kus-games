3. The times are a calculation, not a ruling

Seher computes prayer times astronomically from the position of the sun. It is a calculation aid, not a religious authority. The result depends on the coordinates you provide, the calculation method you pick, the Asr ratio you pick and your device's clock and time zone.

Official calendars published by religious authorities usually add a safety margin (temkin) that varies by region, so the App's output can differ from them by a few minutes. The App provides a per-prayer minute adjustment precisely so you can match your local official calendar. Where it matters to you, follow your local authority or your mosque, and adjust the App to them.

At very high latitudes the sun may not reach the required angle at all, and Fajr or Isha then have no astronomical solution; the App shows them as unavailable rather than inventing a time. Consult a scholar for the rule that applies in that case.

4. The qibla direction is a bearing, not a certified instrument

The App shows the initial great-circle bearing from your coordinates to the Kaaba, and — if your device has a magnetometer — where that bearing lies relative to the phone. Phone compasses are affected by metal, magnets, speakers, cases and vehicles, and can be wrong by a large margin without warning. Use the numeric bearing with a reliable compass when accuracy matters.
