Seher

Prayer times, qibla compass, tasbih · v1.0.0

▶ Try it in your browser — no install, no account, works offline once loaded.

Most prayer-time apps ask for an internet connection to tell you something the sun already decided. Seher computes the times on your phone from the sun's position, so it works on a plane, in a basement, and in a country whose roaming you did not buy.

What it does

  • Six times a day, and a countdown to the next one. İmsak, Güneş, Öğle, İkindi, Akşam, Yatsı — with a progress bar showing how much of the current interval is gone.
  • Notifications per prayer. Turn each one on or off separately. Scheduled on the device, seven days ahead, so they fire with no connection.
  • Qibla compass. The great-circle bearing to the Kaaba, plus the distance. It vibrates once when you are facing it.
  • Tasbih counter. 33, 99 or open-ended, with a progress ring and a short vibration at the target. The count survives closing the app.
  • A thirty-day table — the whole month's six times on one screen, which is what you want during Ramadan.
  • Greeting cards. Ready-made cards for the holy nights, both Eids, Friday and the Hijri new year — pick one and share it to WhatsApp, Instagram or anywhere else.
  • The Qur'anic alphabet, step by step. Letters, vowel marks and reading drills, each lesson ending in a short quiz.
  • Tecvid. Twelve lessons: articulation points, heavy and light letters, the kinds of madd, izhar, idgham, iqlab, ikhfa, the rules of silent mim, ghunna, qalqala and the pause marks.
  • A glossary of 144 Qur'anic terms, with a search box. Free.
  • The ninety-nine names, with their reading and Turkish meaning.

Getting the times to match your local calendar

Seven calculation methods are built in (Diyanet, Muslim World League, ISNA, Egypt, Karachi, Umm al-Qura, Tehran), and Asr can follow either the Shafi'i or the Hanafi shadow ratio.

Official calendars usually add a few minutes of safety margin — temkin — to the astronomical result, and the amount differs by region. Seher has a per-prayer minute adjustment so you can line it up exactly with the calendar your mosque uses, and a one-tap preset that applies a typical temkin.

Cards

The cards are drawn by the app rather than stored as images, so the app stays small and every card is produced at full resolution the moment you share it. Each carries a small Seher mark in the corner.

The messages are written for the app. They deliberately contain no quotations from scripture or hadith: distributing an unverified quotation to a large number of people is not something this app will do.

Learning: alphabet, tecvid, glossary

Fourteen alphabet lessons (letters, vowel marks, sukun, shadda, tanwin, letters of prolongation), twelve tecvid lessons, and a glossary of 144 terms with search. Each lesson ends in a short quiz and your progress is kept.

Everything in this section was written for this app. The reading drills and the tecvid examples are constructed combinations that demonstrate the rule — they are not quotations from the Qur'an.

What the app does not contain: the text of the Qur'an, a translation, a commentary, or recitation audio. Those are other people's copyrighted work, and carrying them without permission would both infringe and make the app look like an official publication of an institution it has nothing to do with. Lessons using the mushaf text will come with a properly licensed source, not before.

The section is a practice aid, not a substitute for a teacher.

Ads and Seher Pro

The app is free and shows an occasional full-screen ad. It will never show one within ten minutes of a prayer time, and never while the qibla compass or the counter is open.

Seher Pro is an auto-renewing subscription (monthly or yearly) that unlocks the locked cards and all of the alphabet lessons, and removes ads. A separate one-off purchase removes ads only. Both can be restored on a new device.

Privacy

Your location is used on the device to compute the times and the qibla direction. It is not sent anywhere by us — there is no account, no server and no login. The advertising SDK is the only third party involved; what it collects and how to control it is in the Privacy Policy.
